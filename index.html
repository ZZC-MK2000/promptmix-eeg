Beyond generic EEG foundation models.
A spatial-spectral encoder for auditory EEG.

A compact encoder trained on six auditory datasets (195 dataset-subject entries, 69,108 eight-second windows, 153.6 hours) and evaluated on NMED-T, which the encoder never sees. The headline is deliberately narrow: only the encoder is target-unseen; the linear and CLAP probes are fit on NMED-T training halves. Under that protocol the encoder beats frozen LaBraM on trial-wise decoding and semantic top-1, while band-power remains the stronger closed-set label baseline. No cross-subject top-1 claim: held-out listeners stay at chance.

Protocol first: most of the gap is the split

We varied only the split on NMED-T, keeping the same windows and band-power features. Random rows (0.896) and held-out blocks (0.873) keep the listener in training; held-out subjects collapse to 0.106. A short-window study reports 88.69% under a within-participant random split (1-s inputs, random time chunks); that is a within-listener number, not cross-subject evidence.

Fig 1 · protocol pitfalls
Minimum controls we recommend. Split by listener, not by window; report a held-out-block split to quantify adjacent-window optimism; include zero-EEG and shuffled-EEG controls; and for any answer-conditioned query, report label-free pairwise and EEG-only variants. Each control changes the conclusion in the cases we examined.

Method: spatial filtering + frozen CLAP alignment

The encoder is 1.14M parameters: K = 32 learnable spatial filters, one-second spectral tokens (five bands), a four-layer temporal Transformer, and a CLAP alignment head. The CLAP audio and text towers are frozen; only the EEG projection is trained. The four alignment terms are window InfoNCE, per-second dense InfoNCE, a song-ID auxiliary, and a subspace placement anchor; a VICReg-style variance-covariance term on the encoder prevents dimensional collapse.

Results on unseen NMED-T

The encoder is trained without NMED-T. The probes are fit on NMED-T training halves (trial-wise) or on other listeners (subject-held-out). Band-power is a strong label-feature baseline; the encoder is not a replacement for it. The encoder's advantage is semantic stability: under subject shift, band-power CLAP cosine drops 0.796 → 0.678, while the encoder changes 0.764 → 0.780.

Ablations and the cross-listener wall

The dense per-second term is the strongest single addition to the CLAP objective. The song-ID auxiliary raises the closed-set number but can select a checkpoint that is not the best for CLAP retrieval, so it is reported separately. The subspace anchor is a geometry control, not a performance claim. A strong within-listener CLAP head still falls to chance on held-out subjects.

What was trained on

Six auditory datasets, all cut into non-overlapping 8-s windows. SparrKULee is speech; the other five are music or natural-sound listening. SparrKULee is mapped from BioSemi64 to the 19 standard 10–20 channels and resampled 120 → 200 Hz. NMED-T is never used for encoder training.

Boundaries

  • Only the encoder is target-unseen. The linear and CLAP probes are fit on NMED-T training halves; this is not a zero-shot pipeline.
  • No cross-subject top-1. Subject-held-out top-1 is at chance for every method.
  • Band-power wins closed-set decoding. We do not claim state-of-the-art classification or a replacement for high-dimensional spectral features.
  • No phase or raw-waveform claim. Raw-patch/codebook pilots are negative and the phase-randomization audit does not support that claim.
  • No data-scaling claim. At 153.6 hours we do not claim data or model scaling.
  • Sparr channel mapping is an assumption. BioSemi64 order → 19 10–20 channels.
