PromptMix-EEG

Brain (EEG) → frozen MusicGen, via mixture weights over a fixed library of native text prompts.
Musicality is guaranteed by construction: every clip is a sample of the untouched generator under one of its own prompts — the EEG only decides which genre to play and how much to blend.

Heard vs generated

Every held-out delivery we have: the stimulus the subject listened to, next to what the frozen generator produced from that same window's EEG.

Why the left column has no audio. The stimulus clips are excerpts of commercially released songs; we can't redistribute them, so the stimulus is shown as a spectrogram only. Everything playable on this page is generated audio. Spectrograms use one common colour scale (−80…−10 dB), one time axis (0–8 s) and one frequency axis (0–8 kHz), so cells are directly comparable.
What the cosine column does and does not show. The comparator is the whole FMA pool (4,132 eight-second clips), not the other 13 stimuli — inside a 14-song corpus two unrelated windows already score 0.869 against each other, so a within-corpus comparator is saturated and cannot show anything. Against generic music the four levels are: our output → FMA pool 0.481 (the pool's own internal average is 0.497, i.e. that is what "generic music" sits at); a real stimulus → FMA pool 0.532; our output → its own stimulus 0.644; our output → a different stimulus of this corpus 0.645. So the generated clips are decisively not generic music — they are pulled into this corpus's neighbourhood, margin +0.163, t = 5.4, 13/14 rows. But the wrong songs gain exactly the same amount (control margin +0.164, t = 6.6), which is what bounds the claim: the pull is to the corpus, not to the individual song. Song identity is recovered by Part 1 (retrieval, below); Part 2 selects a style and stays inside the generator's own distribution.

FAQ

Why is there no stimulus audio anywhere on this page?

The stimuli are excerpts of commercially released songs, so we can't redistribute the waveform. Spectrograms of them are shown instead, and every playable file here is generated. Raw EEG is also not published (participant privacy).

Is the generated clip a reconstruction of what the subject heard?

No — but it is genuinely tied to what was heard at the level of corpus, not song. Against a 4,132-clip FMA pool the generated clips sit much closer to their own stimulus than to generic music (+0.163, t = 5.4, 13/14 rows), so they are not "any random music". The control that bounds the claim: a different stimulus from the same corpus gains exactly as much (+0.164). So the EEG-selected prompt captures the style of this recording set, not which of the 14 songs was playing. Song identity is what Part 1's retrieval delivers (88.2% top-1 held-out).

Why keep the generator frozen?

Fine-tuning was tried and measured worse on a texture-aware metric (MFCC-Fréchet 29,472 vs 3,444), and the corpus is small (14 songs). Freezing means every output is by construction a sample the generator already knows how to make.

Why not interpolate between two prompts?

We measured all 66 pairs. Interpolating the text embeddings puts the output off-manifold for 39% of pairs — the model produces crackle, not a blend. Mixing is therefore done by selecting native prompts, or by blending waveforms after generation.

What is still missing?

A human listening test (MOS) on the generated clips — the one gate we cannot run ourselves. The second open item is geometric: the library tiles only part of the real-window cloud, so the coverage claim is relative to the trivial baseline rather than absolute.

Limitations

  • Small corpus (14 songs, 68 subjects); the genre labels collapse toward the corpus's dominant style, so overall accuracy flatters the classifier and κ is the honest number.
  • Because condition interpolation is off-manifold, the "mix over prompts" is in practice near-single-prompt; the mixture is only safely expressible as waveform blending.
  • Signal metrics (zcr, spectral flatness) do not judge musicality; MFCC-Fréchet helps, but the final gate is human listening, which has not been run.
  • Song-level similarity cannot be measured on this corpus with laion-CLAP: two unrelated stimulus windows already score 0.869 against each other, so the within-corpus comparator is saturated. The FMA-pool comparator fixes that for the corpus-vs-generic question and leaves the song-level question open — a stronger identity metric would need a representation with finer song resolution.
