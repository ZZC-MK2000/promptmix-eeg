Brain signals → frozen MusicGen,
music by construction.

An EEG window can retrieve the heard song, or steer a frozen MusicGen at genre level. The hard part is the listener: zero-shot cross-subject song decoding is at chance; a per-subject fiber estimated from 10 labelled windows lifts it to 45%. The same calibration unlocks song-level description selection (.350) but not the 12-way genre task (shared κ .40 vs k-shot .045). The generator's weights are never touched, so every output is, by construction, a native clip of the original model.

How it works

A CLAP-style two-tower alignment produces the EEG embedding z_win; the same embedding then drives three downstream tasks: song identification, genre-conditioned generation, and song-level description selection. The listener is the bottleneck

We varied only the split, keeping the same 8-s windows and representations. Frozen EEG features decode the heard song at 0.90 within a listener but fall to chance (0.10) on unseen listeners. A clean subject-held-out retraining reaches 0.106 ± 0.020 (14-way; chance 0.071) across five folds; a block-split stock control stays at 0.53 on the same T rows. The split, not the initialization, is the dominant factor.

What k-shot unlocks — and what it does not

The same per-subject calibration that rescues song decoding does not rescue genre decoding. Song-level description selection rises from .103 at k=0 to .350 at k=100 (10-way, 5 folds; chance .10, majority floor .119). The 12-way genre task stays with the shared classifier: κ ≈ .40; k-shot reaches at most κ .045, and matching the Qwen genre prompts by CLAP scores .056 against a .672 majority floor.

Fig 17 · k-shot: song vs genre
Why the asymmetry? The subject fiber is a within-listener transform of the song code; calibrating it adds song identity, not the coarse 12-way genre label. Genre is a shared interface already handled by the mainline classifier — k-shot should not be used there.

Part 2 — genre-conditioned generation

Three measurements decide the design: coverage (can a convex mixture of the library reach real windows), tolerance (is interpolation safe), and which prompt the EEG actually selects. The generator stays frozen because that keeps every output inside the model's own distribution.

The library is saturated. Expanding the pool from 12 to 22 prompts, or re-selecting 12 from 22 on training songs, does not improve coverage on unseen songs (0.830 vs 0.834 vs 0.825; random 12 = 0.814). The trivial target-mean baseline is 0.815, above the codebook coverage, so only the gap over the library mean is informative. The bottleneck is upstream, in the listener.

Limitations & FAQ

Why is there no stimulus audio?

The stimuli are excerpts of commercially released songs, so only spectrograms are shown and raw EEG is not published. Every playable file is generated.

Is the generated clip the song that was heard?

No — this is genre-conditioned generation, not the heard song. The generated audio is genre-level only (+0.103, and −0.005 within the same genre). Song identity is the job of song identification: 84.9% song-level retrieval (held-out segments; subjects remain in training). On truly unseen subjects the calibrated query retrieves real clips at .413 / .504 (bandpower k=10 / 100; chance .10), and k-shot song-description selection reaches .350; genre-conditioned generation remains genre-level.

Why keep the generator frozen?

Fine-tuning scored 7,404 vs 1,389 on distributional texture, and freezing keeps every output inside the generator's own distribution by construction.

Why not interpolate between two prompts?

39% of the 66 pairs go off-manifold: crackle instead of a blend.

What is still missing?

Song-level generation; a human listening test (MOS); coverage is relative to the trivial baseline, not absolute. Free-text captions (LP-MusicCaps, Qwen2-Audio) did not beat the discriminative tags for song selection (.201 vs .350).

  • Small corpus (14 songs, 68 subjects); genre labels skew, so κ — not accuracy — is the honest statistic.
  • The five-fold subject-held-out retrain and the block-split control are single-seed; NMED-T has only 20 crossed subjects.
  • The fiber theory assumes a shared codebook and an isometric/low-rank transform; LaBraM-200 is only partially recoverable (0.391 oracle).
  • K-shot calibrates song-level description selection (.350), but not the 12-way genre task (κ .045 vs shared .40); calibrated generation remains genre-level.
  • Song-level audio generation is not solved: the generated audio is genre-level; the working song-level tasks are description selection (.350) and retrieval of real clips (.38–.50).
  • Free-text Qwen song captions are coherent but generic (.201 vs .350 tags); no title/artist leakage, but the text→audio interface still collapses song identity to genre.
  • The FMA-pool margin measures pull toward this corpus, not song identity (Bollywood stimuli vs a mostly-Western pool).
  • Off-manifold interpolation makes the mixture effectively near-single-prompt, or waveform blending.
  • No metric replaces listening; MOS has not been run.
  • Conditioning is genre-level by design, so song identity is out of reach for the generation arm.
